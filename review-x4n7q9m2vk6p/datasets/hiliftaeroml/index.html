HiLiftAeroML dataset

Dataset overview and leaderboard submission format for HiLiftAeroML.

High-lift aircraft aerodynamics

Overview

A 1,800-case wall-modelled LES dataset formed by 180 NASA CRM-HL geometry variants at ten angles of attack from 4° to 22°, with 300–500 million-cell solution-adapted meshes and native boundary, volume, geometry, force, and visual products.

FluidsBench evaluation schematic

  1. Model inputs CRM-HL geometry + angle of attack
  2. Scoring support Candidate native aircraft boundary and flow volume
  3. Reported evaluation Candidate surface and volume errors, forces, Cp cuts, and near-wall profiles

Technical details

What the source contains

Compressed native boundary and volume VTUs, STL and STEP geometry, force/reference tables, surface renders and convergence plots, fourteen deterministic split families, data-quality masks, a native-point boundary dual-area sidecar for every case, and a provenance manifest for six corrected mean force/moment records.

What FluidsBench currently evaluates

The source now publishes all fourteen deterministic splits, native-point surface area sidecars, invalid-volume-row records, and six documented field-integrated mean force/moment corrections. FluidsBench has candidate surface, volume, force, Cp-cut, and velocity-profile metrics, but its case lists and exact scored array bindings remain provisional and are not active for submissions.

Dataset summary

Geometry
NASA CRM-HL high-lift aircraft with parameterized slat and flap deflections and gaps.
Cases
1,800 simulations: 180 geometry variants across 10 angles of attack from 4 degrees to 22 degrees.
Solver
Fidelity Charles explicit unstructured finite-volume solver with Fidelity Stitch Voronoi meshing.
Fidelity
Wall-Modeled Large-Eddy Simulation (WMLES) with solution-adapted grids.
Flow conditions
Mach 0.2 and chord-based Reynolds number 1.6 x 106.
Mesh scale
Solution-adapted grids between roughly 300M and 500M control volumes; exported volume meshes are octree-based.
License
CC BY 4.0, as stated by the dataset source.

Getting started

Start with the source documentation and split metadata, then download one representative case before planning a full transfer. The commands below pin the source snapshot audited for this page. For leaderboard work, always use the separate scoring source pin and case lists in the FluidsBench benchmark specification.

1. Download lightweight metadata first

python3 -m pip install -U huggingface_hub hf_xet
hf download nvidia/HiLiftAeroML \
  --repo-type dataset \
  --revision bbec30bcfc6103309c1375c5228b3ad0a586bfaf \
  --include "README.md" \
  --include "splits/**" \
  --include "data_quality/**" \
  --include "force_mom_all.csv" \
  --include "force_mom_surface_overrides_v1.json" \
  --include "geo_values_all.csv" \
  --local-dir ./hiliftaeroml

2. Preview one large native case

hf download nvidia/HiLiftAeroML \
  --repo-type dataset \
  --revision bbec30bcfc6103309c1375c5228b3ad0a586bfaf \
  --include "geo_LHC001_AoA_4/boundary_geo_LHC001_AoA_4.vtu.tgz" \
  --include "geo_LHC001_AoA_4/boundary_dual_area_geo_LHC001_AoA_4.npy" \
  --include "geo_LHC001_AoA_4/force_mom_geo_LHC001_AoA_4.csv" \

  --dry-run

The full repository is about 66.8 TB as stored and expands further after extraction. The example boundary archive alone is approximately 12 GB and its area sidecar approximately 546 MB. Remove --dry-run only after checking local capacity. Each sidecar is little-endian float32 in square inches and matches the exact native boundary-point order; a constant area-unit conversion cancels from normalized weighted metrics.

Conditions and sampling

Operating conditions

Each geometry is simulated at ten angles of attack from 4° to 22° in 2° steps, at Mach 0.2 and chord Reynolds number 1.6 × 10⁶.

Sampling

Latin hypercube sampling supplies 180 geometry variants, crossed with the ten-angle sweep for 1,800 CFD cases.

Source correction: mean force and moment coefficients

The 2 September 2026 source release corrects the mean cd, cl, cm, clp, clv, cdp, and cdv values for six cases. The replacements are canonically integrated from the released time-averaged surface pressure and wall-shear fields; the meshes, fields, geometry, split manifests, and the other 1,794 cases are unchanged.

The affected cases are geo_LHC012_AoA_16, geo_LHC018_AoA_16, geo_LHC028_AoA_18, geo_LHC028_AoA_22, geo_LHC129_AoA_4, and geo_LHC172_AoA_22. Their retained *_stdev, *_stderr, and *_ci95 columns are original solver-monitor diagnostics, not uncertainty estimates for the replaced means. See the source correction manifest for the exact old and new values, method, and hashes.

This records a source-data correction only. The HiLiftAeroML FluidsBench scoring contract remains provisional and is not yet pinned for submissions.

Open-reproducibility submission Files, validation and optional artifacts

Check this dataset's current submission status on the leaderboard before preparing a result. Contributions are accepted only while its exact scoring-support release is marked official and open. For an open dataset, contributors propose one result directory at submissions/hiliftaeroml/<submission-id>/ in the FluidsBench submission repository. The planned result package contains:

  • submission.json for model metadata and the required scalar metrics;
  • evaluation-evidence.json linking those submitter-provided metrics to the declared evaluation revision and profile-index checksum;
  • discretization.json describing the training input and supervision supports, inference input, direct model outputs, and mapping to the official scoring support;
  • discretization/cases.jsonl recording case-level counts, domains, mapping, and declared scoring coverage;
  • metrics/cases.json recording the official-support errors, the unweighted and physical-measure-weighted sufficient statistics required by that dataset's contract, and exact coverage for every declared evaluation case;
  • profiles/index.json listing the lightweight profile chunks; and
  • profiles/chunk-*.json containing profile coordinates and predictions for the declared test cases.

FluidsBench uses an open reproducibility track. The declared evaluation partition, scored case lists, and ground truth are public. Evaluation cases must not be used for fitting, hyperparameter selection, or preprocessing statistics. Contributors will calculate the metrics and extract the profiles from their own predictions. Each dataset specification fixes the locations, IDs, weights, targets, and coverage used for final scoring. A model may infer on a different point set, mesh, or grid, but its final predictions must be mapped onto every required entity in the release-bound public field files before the reference evaluator calculates the errors. Large cases may be processed in chunks, but contributors must sum the sufficient statistics across all chunks before calculating a case metric; chunk-level L2 values are never averaged. Leaderboard field errors are calculated on the declared evaluation/test cases, not on the training cases. Full meshes and volume fields are not committed to the submission repository. Contributors may optionally publish example cases or the complete scored predictions in a public, immutable external repository and link that artifact from the submission. Use the HiLiftAeroML benchmark specification for the exact split, metric, case, panel, station, and quantity IDs. The reference equations and code define how the scalar values must be calculated, and the worked examples show the JSON structure.

Before opening a pull request, run python scripts/validate_submission.py --contributor-stage submissions/hiliftaeroml/<submission-id>. The validator checks the schema, split identity, scoring-support binding, derived-score arithmetic, profile coverage, reported discretization, array lengths, ordering, finite values, and file hashes. It checks the submitted package's internal consistency; it does not run the contributor's model or recalculate base metrics from full fields.

A passing validator or pull request will not make a result official. Official results require maintainer approval of the validated submission package. Public code, model, and environment artifacts and their documentation are optional. If supplied, they must use stable links and include the revisions, digests, and licences required by the submission schema; the leaderboard reports those details. Their absence does not change accuracy rank, citation eligibility, or promotion eligibility. Prediction sharing is also optional. When prediction artifacts are shared, the leaderboard separately reports their coverage, artifact-check status, and whether any metrics were recomputed; it does not collapse those distinct checks into a generic “verified” badge. The public validation record states exactly what FluidsBench checked.

Prototype notice: any case IDs, ground truth, profile values, or leaderboard rows labelled prototype are illustrative dummy data. Those materials must be replaced and finalized before the dataset opens; a prototype row is never an official result.

Scoring support and complete coverage Exact files, weighting and complete-case rules

Submissions are currently closed.

Final predictions must cover the complete reference support. For every scored case, this means every required entity, exactly once, in the actual field-bearing mesh or curve file from the named public dataset release. FluidsBench binds those files, their ordering, any masks, and their hashes to the scoring-support release. A method may infer on another representation and may process a large file in chunks, but it must map its predictions back to every required reference entity before calculating the final metrics. Downsampled or partially covered predictions are not complete results.

How to calculate large cases

  • Surface and one-dimensional curve fields use area- or length-weighted relative L2 as the primary value and unweighted relative L2 as the secondary value. Two- and three-dimensional flow domains reverse that order: unweighted relative L2 is primary and area- or volume-weighted relative L2 is secondary. “Unweighted” means that every scored point, node, face, polygon, or cell counts equally.
  • Face- or cell-associated fields use the face area, cell area, or cell volume directly. For PointData or CGNS Vertex fields, FluidsBench supplies the area, volume, or curve length represented by each point or vertex, derived from the same reference mesh connectivity.
  • In one pass over each case, accumulate the squared-error numerator and ground-truth denominator for every relative-L2 variant required in the table above, together with the corresponding entity count and total physical measure wherever one is used. Chunks may be any convenient size, but their numerators and denominators must be summed before taking the square root; never average chunk-level L2 values.
  • Calculate each complete-case percentage first, then macro-average the case percentages so every case counts equally.

Sharing complete prediction fields is optional. A contributor may link versioned predictions in an external public repository, but the submission package itself stays small and records exact coverage plus the per-case sufficient statistics used to reproduce both relative-L2 values.

Metric definitions

Relative L2 error
For each evaluation/test geometry, map predictions to every entity in the release-bound public field support and return predictions and targets to dimensional physical space, \(q^\ast = T_q^{-1}(q)\). Calculate both paired relative-L2 values from the accumulated sufficient statistics: area-weighted L2 is primary on the boundary, while unweighted point L2 (each point counts equally) is primary in the flow volume. Report the complete-case percentages first, then take the arithmetic mean of the geometry-level values.
Relative L1 error
When reported as a supplementary metric, relative L1 uses the same complete dimensional support and the primary weighting for that domain. Calculate a percentage for each geometry, then take the arithmetic mean of the geometry-level percentages.
Dimensional evaluation
Relative L1 and L2 metrics are not computed on normalized, standardized, or non-dimensional training targets. If a model predicts normalized values, the submission/evaluator must undo that transform before scoring. For HiLiftAeroML this means pressure, wall shear, and velocity are evaluated in the dataset-native physical units; Cd, Cl, and Cp-cut comparisons remain coefficient-based by definition.
Surface pressure relative L1/L2
Relative L1 and L2 error for dimensional surface pressure p_surface_pred against the evaluator surface pressure values. Cp is used only for the Cp-cut comparisons and plots.
Surface wall-shear relative L1/L2
Relative L1 and L2 error for the wall-shear vector \(\tau_w = (\tau_{w,x}, \tau_{w,y}, \tau_{w,z})\) on the CRM-HL aircraft surface.
Volume velocity relative L1/L2
Relative L1 and L2 error for the velocity vector \(u = (u_x, u_y, u_z)\) on every field-bearing PointData location in the released volume VTU.
Volume pressure relative L1/L2
Relative L1 and L2 error for pressure on every field-bearing point in the same public volume VTU.
Coefficient of determination
For scalar values \(y_i\), \(R^2 = 1 - \sum_i(\hat{y}_i - y_i)^2 / \sum_i(y_i - \bar{y})^2\). Higher is better; 1.0 is perfect.
Cd and Cl R2
R2 computed over all evaluated cases using predicted drag coefficient cd_pred and lift coefficient cl_pred. HiLiftAeroML also reports pitching-moment R2 in the paper; the current leaderboard stores Cd and Cl for consistency with the automotive tables.
Force R2
Mean of Cd R2 and Cl R2.
Cp cut R2
One global R2 over all selected wing-section surface pressure coefficient samples from the held-out test cases. The evaluator flattens cp_pred and ground-truth cp across case_id, cut_id, station_id, and section sample locations before computing R2. The plotted pressure belt is chosen with the leaderboard station selector.
Cp cut comparisons
Per-case and per-cut R2 values can be reported as profile comparisons, but the leaderboard ranking should use the global held-out score so that cases with low Cp variance do not dominate through unstable per-case averages.
Velocity profile R2
R2 over the HLPW-5 A.1-D.3 profile samples. The leaderboard score is computed on u_over_u_inf_pred, flattened across evaluated cases, all 16 stations, and vertical sample locations.
